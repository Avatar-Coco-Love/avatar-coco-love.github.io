Idea 15 · mini golf · concept

Pocket Links

Mini golf with a new nine-hole course every day, the same for everyone. Drag back, let go, and send your scorecard to the family chat.

  • 9 holes a day
  • 5–8 min a round
  • 7 grounds, one per weekday
  • 1–4 players on one phone
  • phones and PCs

The idea in full

Every morning a new nine-hole course appears, built from the date. Everyone in the world plays the same nine holes, and your score is your strokes against par. When you sink the ninth putt you get a scorecard to share. It looks like a Wordle result: one square per hole, colored by how you did.

Mini golf works for this because everyone already knows how to play it: grandparents, six-year-olds, people who never play games. A putt takes a second to learn, and a good bank shot off two walls feels great at any age. Each hole fits on one phone screen, so a round is nine short moments, each with its own little puzzle.

The course changes by weekday, like a newspaper crossword. Monday's Seaside Pier is gentle, Saturday's Castle Ruins is the hardest, and Sunday's Meadow is a wide, easy course for families. Behind the Daily sits a Tour of hand-picked courses with stars and new putters for people who want more, and a pass-the-phone mode for up to four players.

A round, up close

How a putt works

  • Drag back from anywhere, not from the ball, so your finger never covers it. The further you drag, the harder the putt. Drag back to where you started to cancel.
  • The aim line is a row of dots that stops at the first wall. On Monday and Sunday it also shows the first bounce. Bank shots past that are yours to work out.
  • Fine aim: after you have pulled back, sliding your finger sideways turns the aim slowly. On PC, ← → aim (with Shift for fine steps), ↑ ↓ set power, Space putts.
  • Six strokes at most. If the ball isn't in after six, the game picks it up and writes 7, like a real mini golf course. Nobody gets stuck on one hole.
  • Water costs one stroke, and the ball goes back to where it was hit from.

Scoring

Each hole has a par of 2, 3 or 4, set by test bots (see "How holes are made"). A Daily course's par runs from about 25 on Sunday to 32 on Saturday. The card's squares:

  • ⭐ hole-in-one · 🟩 under par · ⬜ par · 🟨 one over · 🟥 two or more over

Course pieces

Holes are put together from a dozen kinds of piece. Each one changes how the ball moves in one clear way, and each has its own pattern as well as its own color, so nothing depends on telling colors apart.

The week

Seven grounds, one per weekday. Each has its own look and favors a few pieces, so Tuesday always feels like Tuesday. Difficulty climbs from Monday to Saturday, and Sunday is the family day.

Ways to play

The Daily

9 holes · 5–8 minutes · once a day

Today's course, the same for everyone. Your first round is the one that counts for the card. After that you can replay any hole for practice. The last 30 Dailies stay in an archive to play late.

The Tour

8 courses · 72 holes · about 90 minutes

Hand-picked courses with three stars per hole: finish, make par, beat par. Stars open the next course and new putters. Each hole also has one trick shot to find, such as an ace or a bank off three walls. This is where a player who likes the game finds hours of it.

Pass the phone

1–4 players · any course

Take turns on one phone, on the Daily or a Tour course. The scorecard keeps everyone's strokes. A younger player can turn on a bigger aim line and a gentler maximum (four strokes, then the ball is picked up) without affecting anyone else.

Ghost race

from a link · any Daily

Open a friend's ghost link and play the same course with their ball on the green beside yours, replaying their real putts. After each hole you see both scores.

How a day feels

A Tuesday for someone whose sister started a Pocket Links thread in the family chat.

    Seven minutes in the morning, ten at night, and a reason to open the family chat. The Daily brings people back each day; the Tour is what holds someone past ten minutes.

    How it spreads

    The card. Plain text with nine squares, small enough for any chat. People who don't play see a green-and-yellow row and a link, the same pull that made Wordle spread.

    Ghost links. The physics gives the same result on every device, so a link can carry your putts themselves: about two bytes per stroke, under 80 characters for a round. Opening it replays your round beside theirs. It also proves the score, because the ghost has to sink every putt it claims.

    Families. One game on one phone reaches up to four people at the table.

    No accounts, no server. The course comes from the date, and scores travel in the links people send. Names typed for pass-the-phone stay on the phone.

    How holes are made

    A Daily is built from the date. The generator puts together hand-made sections (tee pads, corridors, corners, bowls, islands, gates) and fills them with pieces from that day's grounds. Then test bots putt each candidate hole thousands of times before anyone sees it.

    Rules a hole has to pass

    • Possible: the skilled bot sinks it in 4 or fewer on 99% of tries.
    • Not trivial: the skilled bot's hole-in-one rate stays under 15%, and a straight putt never holes it.
    • Fair to beginners: the novice bot averages no more than par + 2.
    • Par is the skilled bot's average, rounded up.
    • A course needs a mix of pars and at least four kinds of piece, with no two holes alike.

    The bots aim with a little random error, a degree or so for the skilled bot and several degrees for the novice. Without that error, every hole looks easy.

    Tour courses come from the same generator. The difference is that a person plays the candidates and keeps the best ones, so the Tour is hand-picked without being hand-built.

    Tech, phones and access

    • Same result everywhere: physics in whole numbers on a fixed time step, with no trigonometry inside the step, so a putt rolls the same on every browser. A test replays recorded rounds in Chrome, Firefox and Safari's engine.
    • One screen per hole: holes are designed in a tall 9:16 box. In landscape the scorecard sits beside the hole.
    • Drawn in code: flat shapes and patterns on a canvas, sounds synthesized (the putt's tock, the cup's rattle, the windmill's creak). No asset files, as in Foldwork.
    • Offline: installable, and the Daily works without a connection, because it is built from the date.
    • Access: full keyboard play, patterns on every surface, reduced motion (no screen shake, slower windmills), and the scorecard is real text for screen readers.

    Arcade or standalone?

    Standalone, like Foldwork. The Daily, ghost links and the Tour need their own links and saves, and those fit awkwardly inside the arcade's sandboxed frame. The arcade's design rules ask for two or three different verbs per game, and mini golf has one: the putt. Its roadmap also favors improving existing games over adding new ones.

    To borrow the arcade's players, link Pocket Links from the arcade's footer and the projects page. A share card that lands in a family chat should bring more new people than the arcade can.

    A first build plan

    About six PRs. After the fourth, there is a Daily that people can play and share.

    1. Feel: whole-number physics, one hand-made hole, drag to putt on touch, mouse and keyboard, cup and strokes. Hand-play it on a phone before going further.
    2. First pieces: walls of any shape, slopes, sand, water and bumpers; the end-of-hole moment, a scorecard and saves.
    3. The generator and bots: sections and pieces put together from a seed; skilled and novice bots; a balance script with one line per hole.
    4. The Daily: built from the date, the share card, the 30-day archive, offline install.
    5. More pieces and the week: windmills, conveyors, fans, pipes; the seven grounds.
    6. Together: ghost links, pass-the-phone for 1–4 players, sound, and a family playtest.

    Afterwards: the Tour, putters, magnets, ramps and gates. These are mostly new data.

    Risks

    • The putt has to feel right. Everything rests on it. That's why the first PR is one hole and a phone playtest.
    • Generated holes can feel the same. Hand-made sections, seven grounds and the "no two holes alike" rule are the answer. The Tour is hand-picked as well.
    • Different results on different devices would break ghost links. Whole-number physics and the cross-browser replay test prevent that.
    • Crowded genre. There are many golf games on app stores. The difference is the daily card in a chat, with nothing to install first.

    Open questions for you

    1. The name. Keep "Pocket Links"?
    2. One counted round a day, with practice after? I recommend yes; it's what makes cards comparable.
    3. The look. Flat shapes as in these mockups, or pixel art?
    4. A worldwide daily leaderboard? I'd leave it out at first: it needs a server and, with children playing, an age question. Cards and ghosts compare scores between friends without one.
    5. Who playtests the family mode? It needs a real family or class round before it ships.
